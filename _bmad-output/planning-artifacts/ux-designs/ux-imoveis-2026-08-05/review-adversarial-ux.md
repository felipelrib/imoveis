# Adversarial UX Review — ux-imoveis-2026-08-05

Reviewed: `DESIGN.md` + `EXPERIENCE.md` against `.memlog.md` decisions and `.working/source-extract.md`. Lens: where does this design mislead Felipe about reality, waste an evening, or cost him a deal.

## Overall verdict

The spine's honesty doctrine (dashed pending, absent percentile, no fabricated ETA) is real and mostly consistent — but it stops exactly where Felipe's stated worst frustration lives: **availability truth**. The flagship counter-feature (`Verificar disponibilidade` + auto-verify) has no failure semantics, no gone-alert, no verification-freshness display, and a gone heuristic that collapses under scraper outage — so the design can still hand him a confident-looking shortlist of dead listings on a Saturday. Second systemic weakness: two designed journeys (J1 step 4, J2 step 3) contradict the spine's own layout and async-enrichment rules as written.

What genuinely holds up: the honest-absence state table, the map viewport-persistence contract, the percentile *modal sentence*, the per-listing-type price-filter memory, and the anomaly-with-reason-string pattern are all sound as specified.

---

## 1. Trust failures — stale/wrong shown as fresh

- **[critical]** `Verificar disponibilidade` has no defined outcome for a failed recheck. When the recheck hits Cloudflare/403/timeout (the project's own convention says these are `unknown`, and OLX blocks are routine), the spine defines only the happy path ("comes back fresh", J1 step 5). If `unknown` renders as no visual change, the card keeps its fresh freshness stamp and Felipe reads "I checked, it's available" — the exact trust failure the button exists to kill, now with *added* false confidence because he actively verified. (EXPERIENCE.md J1 step 5; Component Patterns has no recheck-result states.) *Fix:* recheck is three-state (`disponível / indisponível / não foi possível verificar`) with its own timestamp (`verificado às 21:14` vs `verificação falhou`); `unknown` must be visually distinct from `available` and must not refresh the freshness stamp.
- **[critical]** Auto-verify finds a shortlisted Property gone and nobody is told. Notifications lists exactly two alert types (new-match, price-drop). When Thursday's daily recheck finds a starred/roteiro item `provavelmente alugado`, no channel fires; Felipe discovers it Saturday morning — or worse, at the door. The highest-value alert in the entire product (a shortlist item dying) is missing from the alert economy. (EXPERIENCE.md Notifications & Recall; J1 step 6 vs J4 step 1.) *Fix:* shortlist-item-gone is a third first-class alert (push + email), on by default for starred items.
- **[high]** Scraper outage mass-manufactures false `provavelmente alugado`. Gone = `sem atualização há 14 dias`, but "no update" is indistinguishable from "scraper broken/circuit-open for 14 days" — a two-week OLX block flips *every* OLX listing to rust gone-state, poisoning the grid and the shortlist with false deaths. Related ambiguity: FR-11 skip-unchanged means "seen but unchanged" — if that doesn't bump the last-seen timestamp, perfectly available unchanged listings rot toward gone. (EXPERIENCE.md State Patterns "Gone listing", "Staleness".) *Fix:* gone heuristic counts only *scrape runs that succeeded and did not include the listing*; skip-unchanged must bump last-seen; when a platform is degraded, its cards show `plataforma sem coleta há N dias`, not gone.
- **[high]** Shortlist shows no per-item verification recency or failure state. J4 step 1 asserts "all availability-verified by the automatic rechecks" — but if Friday night's recheck batch failed (blocked, worker down), the shortlist looks identical to a fully verified one. The one screen where staleness costs a physical trip has no staleness display. (EXPERIENCE.md J4 step 1; Favoritos surface row.) *Fix:* every shortlist row carries `disponibilidade verificada há Xh` / `última verificação falhou` — same honesty stamp the cards get.
- **[medium]** `2 fontes` freshness is per-Property, masking a dead source. Stamp shows one platform (`visto hoje na OLX`) while the QuintoAndar listing — the R$200-higher one powering the J1 negotiation lever — may not have been seen in 10 days. He walks into a negotiation citing a cross-platform gap where one side is gone. (DESIGN.md freshness-stamp; EXPERIENCE.md J1 step 5.) *Fix:* modal's per-platform comparison shows per-listing last-seen; the `2 fontes` badge downgrades (or annotates) when one source is stale.
- **[medium]** Stale verdict beside fresh price after a drop. A price change re-enqueues enrichment (async, AD-4), so for hours the card shows the *old* verdict (`Preço de mercado`) next to a green drop badge computed from the *new* price — two signals from different realities on one card, and nothing marks the verdict as outdated. (EXPERIENCE.md State Patterns covers first-enrichment pending only.) *Fix:* on material price change, verdict reverts to the dashed pending treatment until re-scored.

## 2. Ambiguity traps

- **[high]** `top 25% preço` violates the spine's own hard rule. The rule: "every percentile phrasing must make explicit that lower price = better… wording that could be read as 'top = most expensive' is forbidden." The canonical badge copy contains no cheap-direction word at all — in pt-BR, "top 25% preço" plausibly reads "among the 25% highest-priced." The only place the rule is broken is the copy it was written to protect. (EXPERIENCE.md Voice and Tone, percentile microcopy; DESIGN.md percentile-badge.) *Fix:* badge reads `entre os 25% + baratos` / `25% mais baratos` — the word *barato* must appear on the badge itself, not only in the modal.
- **[medium]** DESIGN.md contradicts itself on gold: frontmatter declares `favourite-star` = `health-warn` = `#d9b35f` ("gold = star = warn — shared meaning, shared ink"), while the Do's table says "Gold star = favourite, **and nothing else**" and warn "is its own amber role." Implementation will pick one at random. Semantically, one hex now means "mine" and "scraper misbehaving," and `verdict-good` (`#d3b662`) is perceptually the same gold — a `Bom preço` underline, a starred photo, and an amber warn dot are three meanings in one indistinguishable hue. (DESIGN.md colors frontmatter vs Do's/Don'ts.) *Fix:* split warn to a distinct amber (more orange, e.g. shift hue ~15°) and fix the frontmatter comment; accept star/verdict-good sharing warmth since both are positive.
- **[medium]** Green drop badge = "buy signal" even on an `Acima do mercado` card. DESIGN.md hard-codes "Green = buy signal. Good news." But −8% on a listing still rust-underlined as above-market is not a buy signal — it's an overpriced listing correcting. The grid scan pattern (hunt green) surfaces exactly these cards. (DESIGN.md price-drop-badge; Colors.) *Fix:* keep the drop badge factual (`−8% desde julho`) but strip "buy signal" framing from the spec; a drop on a rust-verdict card may render in neutral ink, or the verdict must visually dominate the badge.
- **[low]** Gone dots on the map are ambiguous with above-market. When the gone filter is on, a rust map point could be "overpriced" or "dead" — the legend lists four verdicts + pending, with no gone entry. (DESIGN.md map-point; EXPERIENCE.md map legend.) *Fix:* gone map points render hollow/desaturated, and the legend gains a gone entry when the filter is active.
- One line of holding-up: the four-word verdict scale itself (worded, ordered, underline-only) is learnable and sound; no finding.

## 3. Alert economics

- **[high]** Desktop push is the least reliable channel in a localhost deployment, and J2 leans on it. Web push needs the browser running (and a service worker on a localhost origin); when the app tab isn't open — the exact moment an interrupt matters — push silently never arrives. Felipe interprets silence as "no matches" and a fast-moving Savassi 2-quartos goes to someone else. (EXPERIENCE.md Notifications channels; J2 step 1.) *Fix:* email is the guaranteed interrupt channel; push is opportunistic bonus. State this ordering in the spine, or use an OS-level notifier daemon instead of browser push.
- **[high]** J2 contradicts the spine's own async-enrichment reality. New-match alerts fire on ingest; enrichment is async by architecture, so the moment the push arrives is precisely when the modal is most likely to show `análise de IA pendente` — no verdict, no percentile. J2 step 3 ("Verdict, percentile sentence, photos; good enough to keep") is the happy path of a race the design has already declared it loses. The alert's 90-second payoff routinely fizzles. (EXPERIENCE.md J2 vs State Patterns "Enrichment pending".) *Fix:* either gate new-match alerts on enrichment-complete (delay minutes, gain a decidable modal) or design the J2 beat around a pending modal (photos + price + percentile-less triage, with "verdict chega em breve").
- **[medium]** The week he ignores the app: per-search new-match alerts have no batching decision. 40+ matches over an absent week = 40+ single emails (each search×property fires once, but each *fires*), overlapping with the weekly digest covering the same properties — double delivery teaches him to filter the sender to spam, killing J2 forever. (EXPERIENCE.md Notifications & Recall.) *Fix:* daily batch window per search for email; digest excludes already-alerted items or the spine says which system owns which property.
- **[medium]** Drop-alert scope vs since-panel scope mismatch. Price-drop alerts are watchlist/threshold-gated; the since-panel counts "3 quedas de preço" — over what population? All BH? His saved searches? If global, the count is noise he can't act on; if scoped, unstarred saved-search matches drop silently between sessions and the panel is his only catch — with no drill-in (see §5). (EXPERIENCE.md since-panel; Notifications.) *Fix:* since-panel counts are scoped to saved searches + favourites, and the spine says so.
- **[medium]** Per-watchlist drop-threshold UX is deferred, but a *default* still ships. Undecided config UX means week one runs on an invisible global default: too low → alert spam → he disables drops; too high → a R$150 drop on a R$3.000 target never fires. The deferral is acknowledged; the silent-default consequence is not. (EXPERIENCE.md non-designs; source-extract Q10.) *Fix:* even before the config UX exists, the alert email states the threshold that fired it ("queda de R$240 — seu mínimo: R$100") so the default is visible and correctable.

## 4. Operator blind spots

- **[high]** A scraper that stops *running* never turns amber. Anomaly = per-run deviation from own baseline — but a run that never happens (dead Celery beat, both scrapers down from one shared cause: proxy dead, Docker network, disk full) produces no run to deviate. The spine's only signal is the recency string (`26h atrás`) on a still-green dot, with no defined threshold at which recency alone flips the chip. Beat death is a known incident class in this project; under this design the strip stays green while the whole pipeline is dark and every listing quietly ages toward false-gone (see §1). (EXPERIENCE.md Health strip; State Patterns "Health ok".) *Fix:* expected-cadence contract per scraper — chip goes amber on `sem run há Nh (esperado: a cada 6h)`, independent of run-level anomaly detection.
- **[medium]** Cold-start: no baseline, no amber. A new scraper, or one whose baseline resets after a format change/cassette refresh, cannot deviate from a median it doesn't have — its worst early runs (the ones most likely to be broken) show green. (EXPERIENCE.md Health anomaly.) *Fix:* below N historical runs, chip shows a distinct `calibrando baseline (3/10 runs)` state, not ok-green.
- **[medium]** Gradual drift rots the baseline. A rolling own-median tracks slow decay: OLX yield sliding 5%/week keeps every run inside its (also sliding) band; six weeks later yield has halved with an all-green history — and reduced update frequency again feeds the false-gone machine. (EXPERIENCE.md Health anomaly; Admin run-history.) *Fix:* run-history table compares against a *pinned* long-window baseline as well as the rolling median; drift beyond X% over Y weeks is itself a reason string.
- **[medium]** Backfill collision warning lives in the wrong medium. The persistent warning line is on the Admin backfill card — but the collision happens when Felipe types `validate.sh` in a terminal, where Admin isn't on screen. The health-strip backfill visibility (AD-13) helps only if he glances at the browser first. (EXPERIENCE.md Operator Observability, backfill card.) *Fix:* keep the card line, but the spine should note the real guard is the shell-side heartbeat check; the front-door backfill chip (not just Admin) should carry the warning glyph so any glance at the dashboard says "não rode validate agora".
- **[low]** Front-door `Cobertura IA 82%` is an undefined aggregate of per-signal-type coverage (FR-29). If it's a mean, 100% embeddings + 60% visual reads as "mostly done" while the signal he cares about lags. (EXPERIENCE.md Health strip.) *Fix:* front-door % = minimum across signal types, labeled on hover; per-type detail stays in Admin.

## 5. Journey stress (J1–J4)

- **[high]** Two-way bbox sync eats the card he's reading. J1: a drop card catches him → he glances at the map, pans toward the Igreja to eyeball the travel band → bbox re-filters the grid → the drop card vanishes from under his cursor. Mandatory two-way sync with no "buscar nesta área" toggle turns every map exploration into a grid reshuffle mid-thought. (EXPERIENCE.md IA "synced split view"; Map component.) *Fix:* map→list sync is explicit (a "filtrar pela área do mapa" toggle or button, Zillow-style), list→map hover sync stays automatic.
- **[high]** The map bbox is an invisible active filter. Filter bar "shows only active filters" — but a bbox narrowed by an earlier pan is nowhere represented as a chip. Later he widens price, sees mysteriously few results, and has no removable `área do mapa ×` chip explaining why. (EXPERIENCE.md Filter bar; Map.) *Fix:* an active bbox renders as a filter chip like any other, removable with `×`.
- **[high]** Dismiss is "recoverable" with no recovery surface. Mis-click the dismiss on a card (it sits next to the star, top-right on-photo) and the Property vanishes optimistically. The IA has no Dismissed list, no undo toast is specified, and no filter named for it — "recoverable" is asserted, mechanism absent. A fat-fingered dismiss of tonight's best card is an unrecoverable loss as designed. (EXPERIENCE.md Property card; IA table.) *Fix:* immediate `desfazer` toast on dismiss + a `descartados` filter/view; and separate star/dismiss hit targets.
- **[medium]** J1 step 4 breaks the spine's own modal rule. The modal sits on a full-page scrim overlay; step 4 says "Map beside it: travel-time band shows the flat is within 15 min of the Igreja." With the scrim up, there is no map beside it — unless the modal embeds its own mini-map, which no component defines. The journey's location beat is unimplementable as specified. (EXPERIENCE.md J1 step 4 vs Elevation & Depth "Modals sit on a scrim overlay".) *Fix:* either the modal contains a location panel (mini-map with POI bands) or the modal is a side panel that leaves the map rail visible.
- **[medium]** Since-panel counts have no drill-in. "3 quedas de preço" with a single link out means he manually hunts three cards in a full grid — the panel announces news it can't take him to, every single evening. (EXPERIENCE.md since-panel; J1 steps 1–2.) *Fix:* each count is a link that applies a corresponding transient filter (`novos desde a última visita`, `quedas`, `removidos`).
- **[medium]** Comparing two finalists is modal ping-pong by design. The one-level modal rule + "no journey lands in Comparar" means the actual evening endgame — A vs B — is close-modal, reopen, close, reopen, holding numbers in his head. Deprioritizing Comparar is a recorded decision, but no lightweight alternative (pin/peek, star-then-compare-from-shortlist) exists either. (EXPERIENCE.md IA Comparar row; Interaction Primitives.) *Fix:* the shortlist view shows key numbers (price, verdict, percentile, R$/m²) in comparable rows so two starred candidates are comparable without Comparar.
- J4's structure (shortlist → route → post-visit verdicts) holds up, *contingent on* the §1 verification findings and the notes finding in §6.

## 6. Missing-by-omission (beyond the flagged three)

The spine already names export UI, auth/API-key UX, and drop-threshold config as open. Additionally:

- **[high]** "Since last visit" has no session semantics. What resets the panel? If it's "on app open," a 30-second lunch glance zeroes the counters and the evening check — the product's core ritual (J1 step 1) — opens to `0 novos`. If it never auto-resets, counts inflate meaninglessly. The panel's entire value rides on an undefined reset rule. (EXPERIENCE.md since-panel; J1.) *Fix:* explicit mark-as-caught-up action (or reset only after N minutes of active engagement), and the panel states its window: `desde ter, 21h`.
- **[medium]** Gone listings can resurrect and no state exists for it. BH relisting is routine: a `provavelmente alugado` Property reappears in a scrape (often at a new price, sometimes as a "new" listing that dedupe folds back in). Does it un-gone? Does its price history bridge the gap? Does it fire a new-match alert as if new? Undefined — and a relisting of a previously-shortlisted flat is exactly a deal signal he'd want. (EXPERIENCE.md State Patterns "Gone listing".) *Fix:* define a `voltou ao mercado` transition — clears gone state, annotates the price-history chart, and qualifies as alert-worthy for previously starred items.
- **[medium]** Drop-badge baseline is undefined. `−8% desde julho` — since first-seen? peak? last change? A relisted flat that returned at an inflated price then "dropped" to its old price shows a green badge on a net-flat listing. Combined with the resurrection gap above, relist-and-discount games read as deals. (EXPERIENCE.md Property card; DESIGN.md price-drop-badge.) *Fix:* badge measures from a defined anchor (max of trailing 90 days) and the modal chart makes the anchor visible.
- **[medium]** Shortlist notes are load-bearing and undesigned. J4's climax ("banheiro pior que as fotos", `fazer proposta`) requires note entry/edit and a post-visit verdict state machine — no component pattern, no states (visited? proposta feita? recusado?) exist anywhere. The tool "absorbing the day's judgment" is a journey promise with no primitive behind it. (EXPERIENCE.md J4 step 4; Favoritos surface.) *Fix:* define the shortlist row primitive: free-text note + a small post-visit status enum, both editable inline.
- **[medium]** On-demand recheck has no cooldown, and it burns the shared scraping identity. Every `Verificar disponibilidade` click is a live hit on OLX/QuintoAndar from the same IP the nightly scrapers use; an anxious evening of rechecking the shortlist can trigger the Cloudflare blocking that then degrades the whole pipeline (feeding §1's false-gone cascade). A UX button with an operator-level blast radius, unmentioned. (EXPERIENCE.md J1 step 5.) *Fix:* per-listing cooldown (`verificado há 20 min`) + a modest global recheck budget surfaced in the button state.
- **[low]** Semantic search (FR-15) is absent from the IA with no deferral note. Every other cut is listed in *Deliberate non-designs*; search silently has no home — not even a "not this pass" line. (EXPERIENCE.md IA table; non-designs.) *Fix:* one line in non-designs, or a search affordance slot in the top nav.
- **[low]** The Alertas panel is a named surface with zero interior design — row anatomy, read/unread, clearing, retention are all unstated, and J2's "clicks straight into the modal" implies deep-linking behavior nobody specified. (EXPERIENCE.md IA Alertas row.) *Fix:* minimal row spec: type icon, property line, timestamp, click → modal, auto-read on click.
- **[low]** Closing the modal loses grid scroll position — not stated as preserved, and the viewport-persistence contract covers only the map. Mid-grid triage restarting from the top every modal close is death by paper cuts on a 40-card evening. (EXPERIENCE.md Map viewport contract.) *Fix:* extend the persistence contract: modal open/close never moves grid scroll.

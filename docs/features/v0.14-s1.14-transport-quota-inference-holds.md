# Transport-quota inference holds across a throttle — a provider that refused and then went silent stops costing rows their attempts

> Feature branch: `feat/v0.14-s1.14-transport-quota-inference` · Story: `v0.14-s1.14` · Status: implemented

## Problem

v0.13-fu8 taught `GeminiClient` to read a storm of identical transport failures as a provider throttle, so the backfill runner rolls the row's attempt back instead of charging it. The classifier was correct, but the evidence it needs (a refusal the provider *stated*) was in reach far less often than a real throttle lasts. Five ledger entries describe the gaps.

| Entry | What happened | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| DW-12 | A call whose first attempt was answered 429 and whose remaining attempts died as identical resets was declined: any HTTP answer in the call vetoed the inference | the row was charged an attempt, on the call with the strongest evidence of all |
| DW-13 | The licence lasted `ai.gemini_transport_quota_window_seconds` (300 s). A provider that stops answering sends no new 429, so nothing refreshed it | every row attempted after the first five silent minutes was charged |
| DW-14 | The stamp lived on the client, and `--continuous` builds a client per cycle. `backfill.quota_backoff_seconds` (900 s) is three times the window | the cycle after any quota back-off started with no evidence, by construction |
| DW-15 | Recency was one-sided (`first failure - last 429 <= window`). A 429 stamped by another row hours after a storm began made the difference negative | a storm with no refusal near it was read as quota |
| DW-16 | The end-of-run banner said nothing about inferences | an operator could not tell how much of a run rested on a guess |

Three attempts retire a row (`backfill.max_attempts`), so DW-12 to DW-14 quarantined good rows during an ordinary throttle. DW-15 and DW-16 are the other direction: an outage read as quota, with nothing saying so at the end.

## Approach

- **The evidence lives in one process-local object, `TransportQuotaLicence`.** It holds two facts: `refused_at` (monotonic stamp of the last stated refusal, `None` when there is none or a success cleared it) and `answered_since_refusal` (the provider answered something that was neither a success nor a refusal since then). `_run_continuous` creates one per run and every cycle's client is built around it. A single pass, the A/B harness and `create_ai_client` pass none, so the client makes a private one and behaves as before.
- **Why not hoist the client, and why not Redis.** Hoisting the client above the cycle loop changes session and pool lifetime for every run. Redis would need a wall clock (fu8 rejected one: a clock step must not license or revoke an inference), would be a second quota key beside the `backfill:gemma` pacer, and would let a licence survive an operator restart, which is the one moment a fresh look is cheap.
- **Three bases, each anchored on a refusal the provider stated.** `GeminiClient._transport_quota_basis` replaces `_is_transport_throttle` and returns the basis or `None`:

  | Basis | Licensed when | Entry |
  |---|---|---|
  | `in-call` | a refusal was stated inside this call and no success has cleared the licence since; no time test | DW-12 |
  | `window` | the last stated refusal is within the window of the storm's first failure, on either side (`-W <= d <= W`) | DW-15 |
  | `hold` | the last stated refusal is older than the window, at most `ai.gemini_transport_quota_hold_seconds` old, and nothing at all was answered since | DW-13, DW-14 |

- **What a storm is.** Every attempt of the call was a stated refusal (a 429, or a retried answer whose body is a quota refusal by `_is_quota_response`, the test the final attempt always used) or a transport failure; all transport failures carry one full `type: message` signature; at least two such attempts in total; the call ended in transport. A 429 inside the call is evidence, not a veto. Any other answer in the call (a 5xx, a body that could not be read, a 200 whose body could not be read) still disqualifies it. The two-failure floor counts refusals and transport failures together, so `[429, reset]` on a two-attempt client is quota: the call was refused and then cut off.
- **The hold is bounded and cannot feed itself.** It is counted from the last *stated* refusal. An inference never stamps, moves or extends the licence, so one inference cannot license the next for ever. Any answered status other than a 429 ends the hold; a 200 clears the whole licence; a new 429 starts a new hold.
- **Why 7200 s, and why 6 h is the ceiling.** With the shipped values a refusing provider gets four refused passes `quota_backoff_seconds` apart, retry ladders included: 4 x (5 attempts x 120 s `ai.timeout`) + 3 x 900 s = 5100 s, computed from the knobs in `test_config.py` so a change to any of them fails there. After that the runner waits for the daily window anyway. A provider still silent after that wait is past the hold: its storms are hard row errors again, and the circuit breaker or the stall exit ends the run by name. The schema caps the knob at 21600 s so one refusal cannot excuse a dead key, a retired model id or a blocked route for most of a day. `_run_continuous` logs `backfill_quota_hold_not_longer_than_backoff` when an operator's hold does not reach past `quota_backoff_seconds + ai.timeout` (the first failure of a timeout storm in the next cycle); it says nothing when the window is 0.
- **`0` on the window still turns everything off.** A zero window disables all three bases, whatever the hold. A zero hold leaves `in-call` and `window`.
- **An inferred refusal is a quota refusal that says so.** The client raises `AITransportQuotaInferredError`, a subclass of `AIQuotaExhaustedError` with `is_quota_inferred = True`. `is_quota_exhausted` stays the only signal that makes `run_backfill` roll an attempt back. `core.backfill_runner.is_quota_inferred` reads the second attribute (no class-name or text net) and only counts.
- **Rows are counted in core, each row once.** `BackfillResult.quota_inferred_ids` names the rows of a pass whose refusal was inferred (`quota_inferred_rows` is its length). The banner sentence is about rows; the client's `transport_quota_inferences` is about calls and dies with each cycle's client. A rolled-back row is a candidate again, so `_run_continuous` keeps the set of ids over the run: a row classified by inference in three cycles is one row on the banner.
- **Every ending states the number.** One line, `quota inferred from transport storms: N row(s) ...`, on every terminal banner of `--continuous` (complete, stalled, stopped, circuit open, provider refused, lease lost, main thread stalled, and the three migration endings), and on its own line after a single pass. Nothing is printed at 0. The count is also on `backfill_cycle_done` (that cycle), on `backfill_terminal` where that event exists (complete, stalled, circuit open, provider refused; run-wide) and on `backfill_done`. The line printed before a quota back-off (`Provider refused on quota ...; sleeping`) says `(inferred from transport failures on N row(s), no 429 on their last attempt)` when the cycle's refusal was inferred, on all three quota wait lines (the short back-off, the daily-window wait after four refused passes, and the spent local budget). The reason the Operações card shows for exit 10 names both readings (stated or inferred) and both things to check (the quota and the network route); it has no row count.
- **Story 1.13's no-progress rule is unchanged and now better fed.** A cycle that ends on an inferred refusal with nothing enriched sets `BackfillResult.quota_exhausted` and counts towards `backfill.max_no_progress_cycles`. A storm that is not read as quota is a hard row error and does not. Both directions run end to end in `test_backfill_gemma_cli.py`. Inference alone cannot reach the limit of 6, with the shipped values or any allowed hold. After four refused passes the runner waits for the rest of the local daily window; a fifth pass can still be inside the hold when that rest is short; the sixth is a whole 24 h window after the fifth (Story 1.13, `test_the_fifth_and_sixth_refusal_are_a_daily_window_apart`) and the hold is capped at 6 h (`test_no_allowed_hold_reaches_across_a_daily_budget_window`), so the cycle that would complete the count is a row error (the circuit breaker ends the run, exit 9) unless the provider has stated a refusal again. What inference can do is supply up to four, at most five, of the six: one stated refusal, an outage read as quota, and one more stated refusal with nothing enriched after the daily wait end the run with exit 10 where six stated refusals were needed before.
- **Each inference is audible.** Its own counter, a `last_error` of the form `quota inferred from transport (<basis>): ...`, and a WARNING `gemini_transport_quota_inferred attempts=... basis=... window_seconds=... hold_seconds=... signature=...`. `rate_limit_hits` still counts only what the provider said.

### What is read as quota, by case

`W` = window (300 s), `H` = hold (7200 s), `d` = first transport failure of the call minus the last stated refusal.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| attempt 0 answers 429, the rest are identical resets | inferred, `in-call` |
| attempt 0 answers 503 (or its body cannot be read), the rest are resets | hard row error |
| attempt 0 answers 429, another row is answered 200, the rest are resets | hard row error |
| attempt 0 answers 503 with a `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` body, the rest are resets | inferred, `in-call` |
| 429 stated 1800 s ago, nothing answered since, identical storm | inferred, `hold` |
| 429 stated more than `H` ago | hard row error |
| 429 stated 1800 s ago, then a 500 answered, then a storm | hard row error |
| 429 stated 10 s ago, then a 500, then a storm | inferred, `window` |
| cycle 1 ends on a stated 429, 900 s back-off, cycle 2 storms on a new client | inferred, `hold`, through the shared licence |
| a client built without a shared licence | no evidence, as before |
| another row's 429 stamped more than `W` after the storm began | hard row error |
| another row's 429 stamped within `W` after the storm began | inferred, `window` |
| 429, then a 200, then a storm | hard row error |
| `W = 0` | no inference at all |


3. On a host, during a run that reaches the free-tier ceiling (operator):
   ```bash
   grep gemini_transport_quota_inferred <backfill log>     # each line names its basis
   python scripts/dev/backfill_gemma.py --status           # the rows are not in the quarantine list
   ```
   The banner at the end of the run states the number of rows.
4. To turn the hold off, set `ai.gemini_transport_quota_hold_seconds: 0`. To turn the whole inference off, set `ai.gemini_transport_quota_window_seconds: 0`.

## Notes / Follow-ups

- **Deploy.** The runner is a host process: the change takes effect when the `--serve` supervisor is restarted with no run holding the lease. No migration; the new config key has a default. The API image does not need a rebuild for behaviour (`src/core/backfill_runner.py` changed, but nothing the API serves did).
- **Accepted cost of the hold: it masks a real egress failure, and for longer than the hold.** Stated plainly, because this is the failure the inference can hide.
  - *When.* The network route to the provider dies (firewall, DNS, ISP) after a stated refusal and before the provider answers anything else. A throttled run spends most of its time in the back-off that follows a stated 429, so an outage that starts during a back-off is the ordinary case, not a corner. A dead key or a retired model id is not affected: those are answered (401, 403, 404), and an answer is never a storm.
  - *What the run does.* Each pass meets a storm on its first row, reads it as the same throttle (`basis=hold`), charges nothing and backs off `quota_backoff_seconds`. Before this story the first such pass was three row errors and exit 9 (circuit open: key, model id, egress) about a minute in.
  - *For how long.* The inference itself stops 7200 s after the stated refusal. But the fourth refused pass in a row with nothing enriched (45 to 90 minutes into the outage, depending on whether the failures are immediate or timeouts) makes the runner wait for the rest of the local daily window, as it does for four stated refusals (Story 1.13). So an outage of roughly an hour or more parks the run for up to 24 h. If the route is still dead after that wait, the next pass is past the hold: three row errors, their attempts given back, exit 9. A permanent egress failure is therefore named up to about a day late (the hold plus the daily wait), not 2 h late. If the route came back in the meantime the run resumes by itself and what was lost is the rest of that day's quota.
  - *What the operator sees meanwhile.* State `backing-off`; the wait line says `(inferred from transport failures on N row(s), no 429 on their last attempt)`; one `gemini_transport_quota_inferred ... basis=hold` WARNING per pass; `rate_limit_hits` flat. `backing-off` with that clause and no `gemini_retry status=429` line since the first refusal is the signature of this case.
  - *Why the default stays 7200 s.* A shorter hold does not remove the masking, it moves the line: any hold that covers the first pass after a back-off hides an outage for that pass, and a hold that does not cover all four passes turns a real silent throttle into row errors and exit 9 on the third or fourth pass, which is DW-13 again an hour later. 7200 s treats four silent passes exactly as four stated refusals are treated. An operator who would rather hear about an outage within one back-off than ride out a silent throttle sets `ai.gemini_transport_quota_hold_seconds: 0` (window and in-call bases stay).
- **Exit 10 and an outage.** See the Story 1.13 paragraph above: an outage read as quota cannot complete the count of 6 by itself, but it can supply up to five of the six.
- **Host suspend.** The licence is on the monotonic clock. On Windows that clock keeps counting through a suspend (Story 1.13 measured it), so a refusal ages while the host sleeps and the hold is not stretched. Where the clock stops during a suspend the hold is stretched by the time asleep, but a suspend longer than the lease TTL (900 s) ends the run with exit 7 on wake in either case, so the stretch is under 15 minutes.
- **Several runs in one `--serve` supervisor.** `_run_supervised` calls `main` in the supervisor's process and `_run_continuous` creates the licence as a local, so every run starts with a fresh one: a refusal stated in one run never licenses a storm in a later run (`test_continuous_shares_one_licence_with_every_cycle` asserts the second run's licence is a different object). The liveness ticker thread never touches the licence; only the pass's event loop does.
- **A restart drops the licence.** The licence is process-local. A run restarted during a silent throttle starts with no evidence and charges rows until the provider states a refusal again. That is the price of not keeping quota state in Redis.
- **Two clients in one process do not share evidence unless they are given the same licence.** Only `_run_continuous` does that. A single pass after a `--continuous` run in another process knows nothing of it.
- **Concurrency above 1** (the shipped value is 1). The licence is shared by every row of a pass. One row's 200 clears it for a row whose storm is still in progress, an in-call refusal included, and that row is then a hard error: the safe direction. One row's non-quota answer (a 500) ends the hold for every row and every later cycle until the provider states a refusal again; it does not veto another row's `in-call` basis (only a success or an answer inside that call does), which leans towards quota and is left because that row was itself answered 429 seconds earlier. Known gap, low: a storm that began under a hold is judged when its last attempt ends, against the licence as it is then; if another row is answered 429 more than the window after that storm began, the anchor has moved ahead of the storm, neither basis applies, and the row is charged one attempt although the provider has just refused again. Fixing it means judging the storm against the licence as it stood at its first failure as well; it was left out because it needs two rows in flight and costs one attempt.
- **A body that cannot be read counts as an answer, whatever its status.** A 429 whose body read fails is treated as "the provider answered something else": it vetoes the call and ends the hold. Conservative on purpose.
- **Not on the status endpoint.** `last_run` is written by the `--serve` supervisor from the exit code `main` returns (the run is in the supervisor's own process, there is no child) and by a hand-started run's own exit path; neither is handed a `BackfillResult`. Carrying the count there means returning it out of `_run_continuous` and `main`, a new field on the record, the schema, the contract test and the card; the story asks for the banner and its contract says no wire change. The count is in the run's own output (under the host supervisor that is `.run\backfill-host\host.log`, not a console) and in three log events. The card's exit-10 sentence says a refused cycle may have been inferred, without a number. Tracked in the spec's `deferred:` list.
- **A pass that dies on a Redis error after the lease is lost** returns an empty result (`_lease_lost_pass`), so rows inferred in that one pass are missing from the banner count, like its enriched and error counts.
- **Mutation check** (implementation pass, before review): each new branch was removed or inverted in turn and the pinned tests run; 39 of 40 mutations failed at least one test. The survivor, the "every attempt is accounted for" guard in `_transport_quota_basis` (`stated_refusals + len(signatures) != max_retries`, defence-in-depth from fu8), is now pinned by a direct test of that function. The changes of the review pass were each written against a failing test but were not mutation-checked again.
- **AI golden tests.** The gate runs them for a change under `src/adapters/ai/`. Ollama was down when this story was validated, so the suite was skipped and is an operator action: `python scripts/agent/validate.py --only ai` once Ollama answers on `localhost:11434`. What that run proves: `--only ai` runs one file, `src/tests/unit/test_ai_quality.py`, which sends prompts to the local model through `OllamaClient` and compares the scores with stored values. It shows that `adapters/ai/client.py` still imports and that the local path still scores within tolerance. It exercises nothing this story changed: `GeminiClient`, the licence and the classifier are covered only by the unit tests with a fake provider.
- **Not observed on a real run.** Every provider answer in the tests is a fake. A real throttle that goes silent has not been watched through this code; a cloud run spends the operator's quota and was not started.
- **BUG (Low)**: the A/B harness (`scripts/dev/ab_gemini_vs_ollama.py`) passes the window and the hold to its client with no test, like every other line of that script — fix hint: a unit test that loads the script and asserts the kwargs of the gemini arm's client.
